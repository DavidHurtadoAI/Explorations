03 — Getting it into the seabed

Hammering vs. suction: the noise problem

Hammering a monopile is brutally loud — a threat to whales, dolphins and fish. DEME's subsidiary SPT Offshore pioneered suction piles that install in near silence. Compare them.

Suction works by pumping water out of an upturned steel bucket. The pressure difference sucks it into the seabed — no hammer, no vibration, and it can be pumped back out at decommissioning.

Getting the electricity ashore

05 — Rock placement & scour protection

Why we dump rocks around a turbine

Fast currents dig holes (scour) around any structure on the seabed — undermining it. A fallpipe vessel drops graded rock through a 2 km pipe with pinpoint accuracy. Toggle the protection and run the current.

A heave-compensated ROV rides below the fallpipe so rock lands precisely even in big waves and depths beyond 2,000 m. The same vessels cover cables and pipelines.
